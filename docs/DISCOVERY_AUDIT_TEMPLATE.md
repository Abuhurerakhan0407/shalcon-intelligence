# Shalcon Intelligence — Discovery Audit Template

Purpose: turn an interested prospect into a scoped, evidence-based automation opportunity without promising results before the workflow is understood.

## 1. Call outcome
By the end of the call, Shalcon should know whether to:
- disqualify;
- recommend a small pilot;
- prepare a broader system proposal; or
- schedule a technical follow-up because a critical dependency is still unknown.

Do not end a discovery call with a proposal commitment unless the minimum scope inputs below are known.

## 2. 25-minute structure

### Minute 0–3 — Context
- Why did they take the call?
- Which workflow is most frustrating or commercially important right now?
- Who owns that workflow day to day?

### Minute 3–10 — Current workflow
Map the real process in sequence:
1. Where does the inquiry/request start?
2. What information is collected first?
3. Which staff role acts next?
4. Which systems are touched?
5. Where does the process wait?
6. What triggers follow-up?
7. How does a human take over?
8. Where is final status recorded?

Capture facts separately from assumptions.

### Minute 10–15 — Volume + failure modes
- Approximate daily/weekly volume.
- Peak periods.
- Repetitive request categories.
- Common failure or delay points.
- Duplicate/manual entry.
- No-response follow-up.
- Integration failures.
- Sensitive requests that must remain human-owned.

Do not convert rough answers into public ROI claims.

### Minute 15–19 — Systems + access
Record:
- website/forms;
- WhatsApp provider / Meta setup;
- telephony;
- CRM/SIS/ERP/helpdesk;
- calendar/booking;
- spreadsheets/databases;
- existing automation tools;
- API/webhook availability;
- authentication/permission owner.

Mark every integration as VERIFIED, LIKELY or UNKNOWN.

### Minute 19–22 — Success definition
Ask:
- What should the pilot do reliably?
- What must it never do?
- Which metric can be measured before and after?
- Who signs off UAT?
- What would make the pilot not worth continuing?

Prefer operational metrics first: response latency, completion rate, routing accuracy, follow-up completion, staff touches, booking completion, error rate.

### Minute 22–25 — Next step
Choose exactly one:
- Not a fit — explain why.
- Workflow map only — send audit summary.
- Pilot candidate — prepare bounded proposal/SOW.
- Technical validation needed — request only the minimum documentation/access needed for scoping.

Never pressure-close an unclear workflow.

## 4. Qualification score
Score 0–2 each:
- repetition;
- useful volume;
- operational/business impact;
- process clarity;
- integration readiness;
- decision access;
- urgency/timeline.

10–14: priority proposal candidate.
7–9: narrower pilot or nurture.
0–6: do not force a build.

A high score does not override compliance, access or safety blockers.

## 5. Red flags
Do not proceed as scoped if the prospect:
- asks the system to diagnose, prescribe or make another regulated professional decision without qualified review;
- wants Shalcon to use data/systems they cannot lawfully authorize;
- expects fabricated metrics, guaranteed revenue or guaranteed platform uptime;
- refuses a human escalation path for sensitive/low-confidence cases;
- cannot identify who approves the workflow;
- refuses UAT or acceptance criteria;
- expects unlimited changes inside a fixed scope.

## 6. Audit summary sent after call
Keep it to one page when possible:
1. Current workflow.
2. Main friction observed.
3. Proposed future workflow.
4. What stays human-owned.
5. Required integrations.
6. Pilot boundary.
7. Measurement plan.
8. Open dependencies.
9. Recommended next step.

If numbers are included, label prospect-supplied assumptions clearly.

## 7. Proposal handoff gate
A proposal can be prepared only when these are known:
- in-scope workflow;
- channels/integrations;
- required inputs/outputs;
- human escalation rules;
- sensitive-data constraints;
- acceptance owner;
- measurable acceptance criteria;
- client dependencies;
- explicit exclusions.

Anything unknown must appear as an assumption or dependency in the SOW rather than being silently guessed.
